# Thermal Simulations
This project involves developing skills for thermal modelling and analysis within the ANSYS suite for space applications and performing a thermal analysis of an existing CubeSat structure design.

## Background
PAST aims to build and launch a number of high-altitude and space missions. As the thermal environments are complex and vary significantly over time and from room-temperature, an understanding of how a system will respond to different thermal environments is critical to a successful mission. As part of this, PAST needs the capability to simulate the range of thermal environments that a mission will be exposed to.

This project involves the use of the ANSYS software modelling suite to analyse an existing CubeSat structure over the course of an orbit to determine the performance of the design against the space environment. Successful completion of this project will depend on your ability to thoroughly research and understand thermal simulation, the mechanisms and mechanical properties involved in the thermal environment of a CubeSat, and the interpretation of simulation results. 

This project is new to onboarding this year, and PAST mechanical members have limited experience and availability to provide step-by-step guidance on your progress. As ANSYS software is not provided through Curtin but instead through a sponsorship with PAST, you will need to liaise with a PAST member to obtain a copy of the software and any requisite materials . Please only undertake this project if you are (1), willing to undertake significant self-guided learning and (2) have access to a Windows computer with a minimum of 16GB of RAM and 100GB of free storage.

This project has limited capacity and can be undertaken by a maximum of two students at once as we have limited licenses available for the software required.

**Please contact a PAST mechanical member in the recruits discord if you're looking to do this project, as obtaining the materials required will take some time.**

## ☑️ Project Deliverables

### 0. Installation of ANSYS
Install ANSYS Thermal Desktop. To do this, contact Aman Tanday either directly in a project session or through a PAST member in the PAST Recruits Discord. Get this process started as soon as possible, as it may take a week or two. Once you have been assigned a license, you will receive an email to create an account and then be able to access download and installation instructions - once this is completed, you will receive a code from Aman.

### 1. Learning [initial simulations]
Deliverable: Short reflective writing, some visual confirmation of having done learning. Evidence of initial simulation being done and some form of proactive learning.
- Document your learning process - the materials you used and your approach to learning. 
- Perform a basic static simulation of a simple part - one source, one sink, no radiation. Define the part yourself.

### 2. Definition and research of appropriate properties
Deliverable: Short document outlining the materials and properties you care about in CubeSat thermal simulation and a table of the properties used for each material
- Define appropriate radiation tasks and conductive elements if necessary
- Find the materials used within a CubeSat and their relevant properties
- Describe the thermal environment on-orbit, the considerations involved, and sources/losses of thermal energy
- Perform research on and justify the thermal loads & conditions you would want to simulate and environmental factors to account for 

### 3. Static Orbital simulations
Deliverable: Evidence of simulation files being done, screenshots of pre-simulation and results, short description of process undertaken. For this milestone, contact a PAST mechanical member about the design files for an existing CubeSat structure mock-up.
- Either re-model an approximation of the CubeSat geometry (cube, rails, and perhaps a PCB) OR import existing CubeSat files using ANSYS Discovery.
- Define an orbital scenario that would be appropriate for a CubeSat
- Run a thermal simulation to assess the structure suitability based on JAXA requirements (See section 2.4.4)
- Run a thermal simulation to assess structure suitability for launch with no active heating

### 4. [OPTIONAL] Orbital simulations with active heating
- Add in a battery heating element and a pair of 18650 batteries on a PCB to determine heating requirements against typical battery thermal ranges. Perform another thermal simulation.
- Investigate a suitable heating power level and locations to keep all batteries within their operating range.

### 5. Analysis of simulation results and report
- Provide images of all your results
- Analyse the results you obtained, including their implications and how accurate you believe they may be.
- Recommendations for CubeSat design considerations based on your analyses.
- Note down important considerations for the thermal simulation process
- Reflect on how you would approach learning the ANSYS toolkit if you had to do it again.
